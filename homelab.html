Elsewhere

Hardware I own, running software I chose.

Servers, networking and self-hosted services — at home, and set up for other people who'd rather stop renting theirs.

The rack got out of hand, and I'm not sorry.

What started as somewhere to keep drone footage turned into a proper homelab: storage, backups, media, a VPN back to the house, and a handful of services that used to be monthly subscriptions and now aren't.

It's the same instinct as everything else here — I'd rather understand the thing than rent it. And it turns out most people want that too, they just don't want to spend the weekends learning Docker.

What's running

Six things that used to be somebody's subscription.

A streaming service for my own library

Plex serves the media library straight off the NAS to every TV, phone and laptop in the house, and to anywhere else with an internet connection.

Replaces paying to stream a library I already have

Camera recording that stays in the house

Every camera and the video doorbell record continuously through UniFi Protect, with live view and playback on my phone from anywhere. The footage lives on hardware I own, not on someone else's servers.

Replaces a monthly cloud video plan

Network storage

A rack-mount Synology with an expansion unit, roughly 50 TB raw, with the main pool in RAID so a failed drive is a swap and not a loss. Reachable from anywhere, without handing the files to a cloud provider.

Replaces a cloud storage plan, with room to spare

Mac backups

Time Machine backs the Macs up continuously to their own volume on the NAS. A dead drive costs an afternoon's restore, not the files.

Replaces a cloud backup subscription

Uptime monitoring and dashboards

Uptime Kuma checks every service and alerts my phone the moment one stops answering. Grafana, Prometheus and Loki sit behind it for the long view: metrics, logs and trends over time.

Replaces a paid monitoring service

Recipes and meal planning

Mealie. Small, but it makes the point: if a thing is worth a monthly fee, it's usually worth a container.

Replaces a paid recipe app
